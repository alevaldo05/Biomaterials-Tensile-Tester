Uniaxial Tension – New test

Specimen extended at a commanded constant crosshead speed while force and displacement are recorded. Output: engineering stress–strain curve and method-dependent estimates of modulus and strength. Read the protocol.

Safety: the on-screen STOP button is not an emergency stop. Keep the machine’s physical emergency-stop button within reach; it must work even if this page, the computer, or the cable fails.

Governing equations
QuantityEquationDefinition
Engineering stressσ=FA0F: axial force (N). A0: original cross-sectional area (mm2). 1 N/mm2 = 1 MPa.
Engineering strainε=L−L0L0L: current gauge length; L0: original gauge length (mm). Dimensionless.
Modulus estimate (fitted slope)E=ΔσΔεSlope of the linear (elastic) region, fitted by least squares; σ = Eε (Hooke’s law). One value for the region, in MPa or GPa.
Local slope (tangent) estimateEt(ε)=dσdεSlope at one strain ε. Unlike E, it changes along the curve; in soft tissue it rises with strain (J-shaped curve).
Yield strength (0.2% offset)σ=E(ε−0.002)Stress at which the curve meets a line of slope E offset by 0.2% strain. Used for materials without a sharp yield point.
Ultimate tensile strengthUTS=σmaxMaximum engineering stress reached before failure.
Estimated true strain and stress (uniform, constant-volume assumption)εt=ln(1+ε),σt=σ(1+ε)Account for the changing cross-section; assume constant volume and uniform deformation, so valid before necking.
Energy density to recorded endpointU=∫0εfσdεEnergy absorbed per unit volume up to failure strain εf (MJ/m3).

Equations use engineering (nominal) quantities unless noted. Reference methods: ASTM E8/E8M and ISO 6892-1 for metals; this project cites no consensus skin test standard, so preconditioning, hydration and rate are specified per draft protocol.
